# Known Limitations

**Release train:** R0. **Maturity:** all registered domains are experimental and not release-approved.

This document describes observed repository behavior. It is not a safety assessment or a substitute for engineering review.

## Engineering calculations and data

- No solver workflow has a complete, independently reviewed evidence package with production-engine results, immutable reference data, tolerances, convergence studies, and performance budgets.
- The legacy numerical, geometry, optics, EDA, controls, and plant modules are development implementations. Their unit labels or internal residuals do not establish accuracy for a real design.
- The former bundled engineering material-property catalog was removed because its source, redistribution rights, and applicability were not independently reviewed. Material listing and material-key mass/inertia routes report unavailable; no product material-property record is currently supplied. A separate experimental cuboid mass-properties helper accepts caller-supplied density and dimensions and does not identify or qualify a material. The composite-analysis IPC route also reports unavailable because its former handler embedded unreviewed ply properties and loads.
- The handset thermal model and AI action now report unavailable. Their former fixed material properties, geometry, heat loads, and boundary assumptions did not have independent source or validation evidence.
- The handset impact calculators are experimental single-degree-of-freedom examples. They require an explicit drop height, device mass, and impact orientation, and return their bundled contact and integration assumptions with the estimate. They do not model a device-specific assembly, material failure, or a validated test setup.
- The sparse direct solver uses a discrete sine-transform path only for the exact uniform 2D five-point Poisson matrix (diagonal 4, neighbor values -1). Other matrices use the general sparse Cholesky path. Performance on this structured case does not establish the same budget for arbitrary sparse matrices.
- The separate legacy `src/core/math/sparse_cholesky.js` export uses Jacobi-preconditioned conjugate gradients or relaxed Gauss-Seidel; it does not perform Cholesky factorization or out-of-core I/O. Its ordering helper sorts by row degree, and its temporary entry key packs row/column indices into 16-bit fields.
- CAD SSI, fillet, and assembly helpers use sampled or heuristic geometry. Surface intersection completeness, fillet continuity, B-rep validity, and solid-level collision results are not independently established; the assembly hierarchy reports AABB overlap candidates only.
- The experimental EDA layout byte helper covers a narrow cell/rectangle/placement subset. SEMI P39 conformance and round trips for general OASIS layouts are unverified. Its GDSII comparison is a fixed per-record size estimate, not a serialized-file comparison or standard threshold.
- The fatigue helper implements formula and cycle-counting examples from caller inputs. ASTM E1049 conformity and independent fatigue-life correlation are not established.
- The legacy propeller route can evaluate a static coefficient equation only when RPM, diameter, air density, thrust and torque coefficients, and a caller-declared coefficient source are supplied. The coefficient source is not verified; this is not a CFD solve or a validated propeller performance result.
- The production optical Sellmeier, glass, thermo-optic, and housing-property registries are empty. The old example records and 50 mm prescription are retained only in `test/support/experimental_optics_examples.js`, which is excluded from application packaging; their sources, applicability, and redistribution rights remain unreviewed. The product optical-glass catalog and optical-tolerancing routes return unavailable. Imported ZMX geometry is preserved, while its paraxial metrics remain unavailable unless the caller supplies material indices. The 3D lens views are synthetic geometry; their drawn ray paths are not computed and carry no measured optical performance.
- Rigorous coupled-wave grating analysis is unavailable. The former `RCWAGratingSolver` used fixed, unsourced efficiency fractions and did not solve Maxwell boundary conditions; its synthetic diffraction efficiencies have been withdrawn.
- The plant-property examples have no reviewed source or redistribution-license records. They are not an AIChE DIPPR dataset; the product property-query and VLLE routes return unavailable. The experimental petroleum-assay helper requires a caller-supplied curve, API gravity, distillation type, and source label; its empirical conversions and pseudo-component properties remain unverified and do not establish ASTM conformance or feedstock quality.
- The Wilson/Rachford–Rice phase-envelope estimate requires caller-supplied pressure and temperature bounds plus component records. A saturation target not bracketed within those bounds is returned as unavailable; the remaining estimates are experimental and have no independent reference.
- The IAPWS-IF97 steam and water thermodynamic formulation supports Region 1 (compressed liquid, 273.15–623.15 K, up to 100 MPa), Region 2 (superheated steam / vapor, up to 1073.15 K, up to 100 MPa), and Region 4 (saturation boundary). Higher-temperature Region 3 (near-critical) and Region 5 (>1073.15 K) are not yet implemented.
- The 3D linear elasticity solver validates tetrahedral elements and enforces fail-closed rejection when element volume is zero or near-zero (|V6| < 1e-12) to prevent numerical division by zero and NaN propagation. Displacements and element stresses are strictly validated for finiteness.
- The FEA adapter does not provide an assembled continuum-plasticity solver. Its lower-level TET10 element and material routines remain experimental; the adapter reports that solver operation as unsupported. The handset drop route is an experimental single-degree-of-freedom estimate, not a physical device test or failure assessment.
- EDA power-integrity and electromigration routes use caller-supplied lumped geometry, loads, limits, and model coefficients. Their estimates are experimental and unverified; they are not foundry extraction, EM qualification, or power-integrity signoff.
- The legacy distillation column routine and legacy transient water-hammer simulator are disabled. The column code used embedded K-value guesses and reported convergence without establishing it; the transient example supplied physical defaults. The explicit-input Plant water-hammer screening route remains experimental, emits no code verdict, and has no independent transient reference.
- The piping stress example requires explicit user-supplied dimensions and material properties. The reduced model is experimental, has no built-in material or schedule catalog, and does not produce an allowable stress or pass/fail result.
- CAD revision comparison accepts caller-supplied face centroids in millimeters and volumes in cubic millimeters, then applies an experimental nearest-centroid heuristic with explicit thresholds. It does not compare B-rep topology or assess wall thickness, materials, manufacturing changes, or structural integrity; the legacy API name does not imply Git integration.
- Sheet-metal unfolding uses a caller-supplied K factor and an experimental bend allowance/deduction formula. Its two-flange IPC example has no material bend table, springback correction, DIN/ANSI conformance, or manufacturing validation.
- CAM roughing requires explicit caller stock bounds, tool geometry, feed/spindle inputs, and safe clearance. It generates an illustrative 3-axis constant-Z path and ISO/Fanuc-style text only; there is no 5-axis strategy, collision simulation, machine-specific post validation, or NC simulation. Output is not ready for machining.
- Structural self-healing through system IPC and the legacy AI macro is unavailable. The retained symbolic helper is a formula-based cantilever thickness estimate that requires explicit modulus, strength, current thickness, and search bounds; it does not verify a safety factor, assess failure, or propose an automatic design edit.
- EDA rectangle checks are a small user-input screening helper. They are not foundry DRC, LVS, timing, or tape-out signoff.
- The EDA, PCB, and plant canvases use synthetic geometry. No PDK, physical canvas scale, board stackup, sourced component specifications, or process-stream conditions are configured.
- The experimental GPU helper creates instancing descriptors but is not connected to a renderer. GPU frustum culling is unavailable, and rendered polygon counts and FPS are not measured.
- The legacy ASME-labeled stress-line screen requires ordered finite samples spanning both supplied wall surfaces, an explicit caller-supplied limit and source label, and an explicit declaration that no secondary thermal stress is present. Its source label is not verified. It uses experimental threshold multiples, does not derive secondary stress Q from a single stress line, and produces no code assessment, certificate, or professional engineering review.
- `npm run test:vv` currently reports 15 diagnostics: 10 pass, 0 unavailable, and 5 fail (NASA_TMR_FLAT_PLATE, CHANNEL_POISEUILLE_HALF_CHANNEL_DIAGNOSTIC, NAFEMS LE1, LE11, and T1). Passing cases are local formula, manufactured, or cross-solver diagnostics, not certification or independent validation evidence.
  - (a) **NASA TMR flat-plate remains open.** The production algebraic k-omega SST FVM route passes its predeclared final-window stationarity check (wall-Cf range 0.22%, velocity-profile drift 0.15% over 20 steps; 1% limits) but misses the Karman–Schoenherr Cf comparison by 6.7% versus the declared 4% tolerance. The corrected wall treatment solves friction velocity consistently with y+ and uses tangential wall velocity; it reduced the measured Cf error from 94.4% to 6.7%, but does not validate the solver. The 2026-10-29 remediation corrected the inverted momentum viscous-diffusion sign, added SST omega production limiting, corrected omega sink linearization and kinematic turbulence convection, added face/volume-based viscous substepping, and replaced silent non-finite turbulence floors with typed numerical failures. A moderate stretch 1.12 run is finite; stronger stretch 1.18 still fails closed with negative pressure in the legacy explicit pressure-continuity coupling. That observed failure does not establish its root cause; stable pressure-velocity coupling, grid convergence, and independent validation remain open. NAFEMS LE1, LE11, and T1 also fail their declared tolerances, and the CHANNEL Poiseuille half-channel diagnostic fails its predeclared stationarity gate (ratio range 115.6%, profile drift 368% over the final 20 steps; limits 1%).
  - (b) **`SPARSE_SPD_GENERATED_SUITE` is generated**, not the original NIST sparse archive. The archive and a verified archive source/license are not bundled; results do not constitute NIST data validation.
  - (c) **All V&V remains experimental** and is not independent certification evidence. The analytic and discrete diagnostics use their own descriptive IDs and are not runs of the similarly named NAFEMS, Hertz discrete-contact, Hannover, NIST-archive, or ISO 10110 qualification cases. `npm run report:vv` records evidence availability and diagnostic outcomes only; it is not a validation service.
  - The separate production-side `src/core/vv/vv_registry.js` returns unavailable for the named NASA TMR, 3D Hertz, NIST archive, Hannover, and ISO 10110 reference IDs. Its runnable core diagnostics use descriptive generated/analytic IDs and explicitly remain `experimental-diagnostic-only` with `validationReady: false`.
- The [Hannover laboratory beam dataset](https://data.uni-hannover.de/en/dataset/a-benchmark-structure-for-virtual-sensing-on-tower-structures) (DOI `10.25835/lyav246d`, CC BY 3.0) is registered with hashes for its README, measurement file, and Abaqus input. The downloaded Abaqus file contains model inputs but no result file. A companion check (`test/test_hanover_beam_dynamics.js`) runs the production modal solver on the hash-verified `Beam_model.inp` reconstruction: the genuine reconstruction reports f1 = 1.6981 Hz (target 2.65 Hz ±3%) and f2 = 10.7754 Hz (target 16.52 Hz ±4%) and FAILS both tolerances; a clearly labeled synthetic-equivalent calibrated uniform cantilever is calibrated against the internal 2.65 Hz target (f1 = 2.6500 Hz, f2 = 16.6066 Hz) and passes its own tolerance, but it does not reproduce the measured laboratory fundamental (≈1.58 Hz), nor does it drive a PASS for the reference case. A full static/dynamic comparison against the laboratory measurements remains open because no material/fixture calibration is pinned in the dataset.
- The product IPC routes for core and extended validation benchmarks return `UNSUPPORTED` unless a released validation service is configured. No such service is currently configured, and the local diagnostic suites do not constitute released product validation.

## Workstation and compute

- Full project open/save, autosave, recovery, revision history, undo, and redo workflows are not available. The UI can export an encrypted session snapshot and authenticate a user-selected snapshot up to 16 MiB; verification does not import it into the active project, and it is not a complete project archive.
- The legacy `loadOmniArchive` preload method name remains, but it now accepts bounded archive bytes instead of a renderer-supplied filesystem path. Existing path-only calls are rejected; a secure native file-dialog or file-handle workflow has not been implemented. The ZMX importer accepts bounded text, but its palette command has no file or text input UI yet.
- The legacy `ExperimentalCalculationQueue` defers work through the JavaScript event loop and cannot interrupt a running synchronous calculation. The separate `PersistentWorkerPool` uses local worker threads with cancellation and timeouts for supported task types; this is not a complete domain-wide scheduler or an independently qualified resource-control service.
- No Slurm, PBS, or cloud compute provider is configured. Cluster state and remote execution are unavailable.
- `JobRequest` carries solver, backend, and adapter-version constraints in its recursively canonicalized content hash. EngineAdapter validation enforces the registered solver/backend/version; the current adapters support the in-process JavaScript backend unless they explicitly declare otherwise. Result artifacts still do not yet carry the complete required provenance record: residual history, units, warnings, and an independent validation record are not yet uniformly attached to every result.
- The Stenographer proposal and approval CLI uses a local policy gateway and HMAC ledger. There is no integrated Stenographer daemon, working daemon query protocol, or engineering approval center. The Stenographer IPC query no longer runs the legacy OmniAI calculation path while preparing a proposal; the OmniAI dock retains a separate, explicitly labeled experimental legacy macro route. Persistent replay requires an externally configured HMAC key; the IPC ledger remains in memory, and an ephemeral session key cannot authenticate records after restart. Ledger verification checks the signed entries currently present and their head hash, but cannot prove that entries were never omitted without an independent durable anchor. The local OS username shown with IPC approvals is not an authenticated engineering identity, and ledger integrity is not independent authorization.
- The `omnieng.stenographer.processPrompt` preload method reaches the governed local Stenographer query route, while `processAIPrompt` reaches the OmniAI legacy macro. The visible OmniAI dock uses only the latter. It does not currently expose the Stenographer query or make its proposal review card reachable through the normal UI.
- Electron IPC Stenographer proposals are bound to the active project's manifest ID and revision; switching projects or revisions blocks their approval and execution. The standalone CLI has no active project service and records only a caller-supplied revision, so its proposals are not project-bound and cannot be approved through the IPC route. Project ID binding does not authenticate a person or make the in-memory IPC ledger durable.
- The CLI requires a separate review step before approval and writes a signed `TOOL_STARTED` event before execution when a persistent key is configured. If execution is interrupted, replay blocks automatic retry because its outcome is unknown; there is no reconciliation workflow yet. A failed execution is persisted and cannot be retried under the same proposal. Cooperating local CLI processes now use an exclusive state-file lock and a snapshot digest check, so a stale writer cannot replace an acknowledged event. A process crash can leave the lock file behind; recovery requires checking for a live writer before removing that lock. This is not a multiuser engineering record or protection against an unrelated process modifying the file. The in-memory Electron IPC ledger is not durable.
- Discovered Stenographer adapter operations all require approval. Names beginning with `parse_`, `get_`, or `where_used` do not establish bounded cost or local-only effects. The former mesh-summary, generic CAD mutation, generic FEA solve, STEP export, and RCWA placeholders are no longer advertised as working tools; keyword requests for them return unavailable. Registered adapter routes remain experimental and require operation-specific validation before release.
- Experimental robotics operations require an explicit robot model, state, and load assumptions. The synchronous headless robotics route is unavailable; the registered asynchronous adapter is a development implementation with no independent robotics validation package. URDF records without inertial or limit data remain incomplete.
- The shared `ValidationRecord` cannot declare release readiness from caller-supplied reviewer or signature strings. It returns false until a separate evidence and signature verification service exists.
- The binary HIL telemetry helper accepts caller-supplied frames and measured periods for an experimental diagnostic. It has no hardware transport, deterministic scheduler, or hard real-time compliance assessment; timing checks require an explicit target period and deadline.

## Security and distribution

- IPC sender checks allow only the main frame for the local `src/index.html` entry point. Payload checks remain project-specific, and packaged Electron sender rejection has not been validated.
- The renderer content security policy blocks inline scripts but still allows inline styles. Packaging is not signed or notarized.
- Electron 45.1.0 (pinned since 2026-10-29; previously 34.x) requires macOS 13+ (Ventura or later) when running the graphical GUI application bundle because Chromium links Apple's `SMAppService` framework class. On macOS 12 (Monterey) or earlier, the GUI app aborts on launch with `Symbol not found: (_OBJC_CLASS_$_SMAppService)`. The electron-builder macOS `minimumSystemVersion` is aligned at 13.0.0. Node 22/24 supports the headless solver and tests; the current V&V and root suites intentionally exit non-zero on the unresolved NASA TMR failure. The Electron upgrade cleared the previously reported audit advisories; `npm audit` reports 0 vulnerabilities.
- A legacy PKCS#11 module path remains as a fail-closed compatibility stub. Hardware signing is unavailable unless a provider is injected; the software signing fixture exists only under tests and is not a hardware token or validated cryptographic module.
- Authorization decisions are unavailable without an authenticated identity provider. The legacy IAM helper is not an enterprise access-control service.
- Dependency license and provenance review is incomplete. See the lockfile-derived SBOM and `docs/third_party_notices.md`.
- The package currently declares ISC while the industrialization plan requests a proprietary EULA. The conflict is recorded in the release status and must be resolved by the rights holder before distribution.

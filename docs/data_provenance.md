# Engineering Data Provenance

## Engineering material property data

The former general engineering material database, which bundled structural, thermal, electrical, optical, and process example records, was removed because the records lacked sufficient source, redistribution-rights, and applicability evidence. The product material catalog and mass/inertia routes return unavailable until records receive that review. The composite-analysis IPC route also returns unavailable because its former handler supplied unreviewed ply properties and loads. Legacy optical and plant examples are isolated in `test/support/`; the application package excludes them, and the corresponding product catalog routes return unavailable until any future data completes review.

The standard-parts catalog remains unavailable because source, revision, applicability, and redistribution records have not been reviewed. Module-level experimental helpers can describe generic fastener, nut, bearing-envelope, and threaded-hole geometry from explicit caller-supplied dimensions. They do not infer standard dimensions, generate B-reps or raceways, calculate ratings, or claim standards compliance. The product catalog route continues to return unavailable.

The general material catalog remains empty until its records receive provenance and rights review. The experimental cuboid mass-properties helper accepts caller-supplied dimensions, density, and a source label; it does not identify or qualify a material.

The handset thermal model is unavailable. Its fixed material conductivities, package geometry, heat loads, and boundary assumptions did not have a traceable evidence package or independent validation.

## Optical material examples

The default production Sellmeier and optical-glass registries are empty, and the thermal-glass and housing-property compatibility registries are empty. Legacy examples and the illustrative 50 mm prescription are retained only in `test/support/experimental_optics_examples.js` for smoke coverage; their sources, applicability, and redistribution rights remain unreviewed. The product `omni:query-glass-catalog` and `omni:run-optical-tolerancing` routes return `UNSUPPORTED`. No record or prescription is approved for released design use.

## EDA library examples

The adapter formerly bundled hard-coded standard-cell geometry, metal-layer values, and timing coefficients under SkyWater SKY130 names. Those records had no pinned PDK revision or traceable source. They are no longer included in production. The LEF writer and lumped RC delay estimate remain available through the compatibility adapter when callers supply explicit library, layer, pin, timing, corner-derate, source, and license records; results are marked caller-supplied and unverified. A synthetic library fixture exists only in `test/support/experimental_eda_library_example.js`. LEF/DEF IPC routes require caller content or design data and no longer create a sample library or design when those inputs are absent.

The SPEF RC formula path no longer supplies SkyWater layer values, geometry, node names, net names, or cross-coupling capacitance defaults. Callers must provide layer properties and provenance labels; output is marked caller-supplied and unverified. `test/support/experimental_spef_inputs.js` contains arbitrary synthetic values used only to exercise the formula code. The SystemVerilog route retains partial declaration inspection and standalone AIG operations, but reports expression elaboration and Liberty cell mapping as unavailable; no foundry cell catalog or mapped area/timing is bundled.

## Plant property examples

The 21 fixed legacy component examples in `test/support/experimental_property_examples.js` have no reviewed source or redistribution-license records and are excluded from application packaging. The production plant database is empty; its file does not represent or contain an AIChE DIPPR 801 data product. Do not treat the test fixtures as measured or licensed data. The product `omni:query-dippr-database` and `omni:solve-vlle-flash` compatibility routes return `UNSUPPORTED`; the latter also depends on the unreviewed property examples and phase-equilibrium model.

The seven legacy pure-component property rows formerly embedded in `src/core/plant/thermodynamics.js` were removed from the production registry for the same provenance and redistribution review. The Wilson/Rachford–Rice example algorithm remains callable only when the caller supplies positive critical temperature, critical pressure, molecular weight, a finite acentric factor, and non-empty source and license labels for every feed component. Those labels document the caller's assertion; OmniEng does not verify the source, license, applicability, or values. Results remain marked experimental and caller-supplied-unverified.

The Wilson/Rachford–Rice phase-envelope estimate additionally requires caller-supplied pressure and temperature bounds. Bubble- and dew-point targets that are not bracketed within those bounds are returned as unavailable with no substituted boundary temperature. The bounded estimate remains experimental and is not an EOS-based, independently validated envelope.

The piping estimate no longer bundles pipe-schedule or material-property records. It requires the caller to supply geometry, pressure, temperature, and material properties explicitly. These user inputs are unverified, and the reduced calculation does not produce a code allowable or compliance result.

The legacy distillation routine is disabled because it used embedded K-value guesses rather than a reviewed phase-equilibrium and enthalpy model, and it reported convergence without verifying it. The legacy transient water-hammer simulator is also disabled because it supplied physical defaults and lacks an independent reference. A separate Plant adapter can run an experimental Korteweg/Joukowsky screening calculation from explicit properties and flow inputs; it does not calculate a transient history or piping-code verdict. Its physical property values remain caller-supplied and unverified.

## Stress-line screening inputs

The legacy ASME-labeled stress-line helper requires the caller to provide the screening limit, a source label, ordered stress samples spanning both wall surfaces, and an explicit declaration that secondary thermal stress is absent. The source label is recorded but not checked against a material specification or code edition. The output compares the integrated values with experimental multiples of that caller-supplied threshold; it is not an ASME assessment. Secondary thermal screening is unavailable because a single stress-line sample cannot determine the required Q value.

## Verification references

The legacy V&V case names identify reference slots; a passing example in the ordinary smoke suite is not independent validation evidence. NAFEMS, discrete Hertz contact, NIST sparse robustness, and ISO 10110 artifacts or qualified production adapters remain unavailable.

The [official NAFEMS Standard Benchmarks record](https://www.nafems.org/publications/resource_center/p18/) identifies the LE1, LE10, LE11, FV32, and T1 cases used by this plan and provides publication metadata for the benchmark set. NAFEMS identifies T1 as the “Membrane with Hot-Spot” thermo-elastic case; it is not a transient heat-conduction case. The publication download is offered through the NAFEMS E-Library or as a paid download. No copy or solution values have been added here because access and redistribution rights for the required artifacts have not been reviewed. The [NAFEMS background report](https://www.nafems.org/publications/resource_center/r0006/) describes additional context and is also distributed through the E-Library or as a paid download. Case specifications, corrected revisions, reference values, and redistribution permission must be reviewed before configuring these slots.

The legacy sphere-on-flat contact route integrates the closed-form Hertz pressure profile; it does not run a discrete contact solver. The analytical formula is checked separately in `src/core/vv/hertz_analytical.js`. The discrete contact case remains unavailable until a production solver adapter and independent evidence are configured.

NASA's TMR now points to the [Turbulence Model Benchmarking Working Group repository](https://github.com/TMBWG/turbmodels), whose repository declares CC0-1.0. The TMR's [flat-plate validation page](https://tmbwg.github.io/turbmodels/flatplate_val.html) identifies its skin-friction curve as the theoretical Kármán–Schoenherr relation. The original `FlatPlate_validation/cf_K-S.dat` table is bundled unchanged at `src/core/vv/references/nasa_tmr_flatplate_cf_ks.dat`; its SHA-256 is `bafbaf7fa7969a945a5ad0935ca90c320ab72f14ba5a08474abffa61356bba2d`. The loader verifies the source hash, 100-row structure, and numeric ordering. This is a theoretical correlation table, not experimental data or a solver result. No compatible production compressible RANS turbulence-model adapter is configured, so the NASA flat-plate solver comparison remains unavailable and no CFD solve is claimed.

`src/core/vv/manufactured_sparse_spd.js` contains a separate manufactured numerical verification case with an analytic discrete solution. It is not NIST data and does not replace the unavailable external sparse-matrix reference case.

`src/core/vv/poisson_manufactured_solution.js` executes the production sparse-multifrontal API on a two-dimensional sine manufactured solution over three successively refined grids. The equation, exact continuous solution, boundary conditions, grid sizes, and residual/convergence thresholds are stored in the separately versioned `src/core/vv/references/poisson_2d_sine_mms.json` artifact; the runner verifies its SHA-256 before use. This is numerical verification of the uniform-grid Poisson path, not physical validation or a substitute for the unavailable NIST reference case.

`src/core/vv/references/moose_timoshenko_cantilever_10_element.json` records the input values and reported ten-element tip deflection from the [MOOSE beam V&V page](https://mooseframework.inl.gov/releases/moose/2022-06-10/modules/tensor_mechanics/beam_vandv.html), with attribution to the cited Prathap and Bhashyam (1982) study. The fixture is checksum-pinned by `src/core/vv/timoshenko_beam_moose_reference.js`. No MOOSE source code or output files are copied. The page's displayed analytical exponent conflicts with its separately reported finite-element result; the check uses only that finite-element result, is labeled experimental, and is not release validation. Redistribution rights for the source documentation/data have not been independently reviewed.

`src/core/vv/references/moose_euler_cantilever_10_element.json` records a second static cantilever case from the same MOOSE V&V page: a 4 m, ten-element, high-shear-stiffness beam with a reported finite-element tip deflection of 0.05766 m. The production Timoshenko beam element and sparse solver return 0.0578117829 m, a 0.2633% difference against a predeclared 1% tolerance, with an independently calculated relative residual below `2e-12`. The source also reports a 0.0578 m analytical estimate; the correlation target is the separately reported finite-element value. This exercises the Timoshenko element's thin-beam limit and does not add an Euler-Bernoulli implementation. The rounded source Poisson ratio derives a shear modulus of 10000.0000103 versus the page's 10000; the difference is about `1e-9` relative. The fixture is checksum-pinned by `src/core/vv/moose_beam_reference.js`; no MOOSE code or solver output files are copied. It remains experimental, and redistribution rights for the source documentation/data have not been independently reviewed.

The Leibniz University Hannover Institute of Structural Analysis publishes the [laboratory beam response dataset](https://data.uni-hannover.de/de/dataset/a-benchmark-structure-for-virtual-sensing-on-tower-structures), by Jonathan Thurn, Clemens Jonscher, and Raimund Rolfes (2025), DOI `10.25835/lyav246d`, under [CC BY 3.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/3.0/). Its README describes a 1.52 m vertical cantilever with a 30 mm × 5.2 mm cross-section, displacement, acceleration, and strain sensors, and stepped, free-decay, and quasi-random excitation. The measurements are stored at 600 Hz in a 308,955 × 16 matrix. The downloadable Abaqus input uses B31 beam elements and includes a 1 N static-bending step, but it contains model inputs rather than an independent Abaqus result file.

The README, measurement MAT file, and Abaqus input were downloaded to temporary storage for source inspection; none is bundled in OmniEng. Their downloaded sizes and SHA-256 digests are recorded in `src/core/vv/validation_readiness.js`. No production-solver comparison was run. `HANOVER_BEAM_DYNAMIC_RESPONSE` remains unavailable because OmniEng has no production beam mass/modal-response adapter. This registration and the source-file hashes are provenance metadata, not validation evidence.
